Olympus FV3000

Confocal laser scanning microscope for multichannel and live-cell imaging

A versatile confocal imaging platform for multichannel fluorescence imaging, live-cell microscopy, three-dimensional imaging, and fluorescence dynamics.

Confocal Live-Cell Silicone Oil Multichannel FRAP
Technology

Imaging Capabilities

The FV3000 combines laser scanning confocal microscopy with sensitive fluorescence detection and flexible multichannel imaging, supporting a wide range of biological applications.

Confocal Imaging

Optical sectioning for high-resolution fluorescence imaging of cells, tissues, and biological structures.

Multichannel Fluorescence

Acquire multiple fluorescence channels for localization, colocalization, and multicolor imaging experiments.

Spectral Detection

Spectral detection and fluorescence separation support multicolor experiments with overlapping emission signals.

Live-Cell Imaging

Time-resolved fluorescence imaging for monitoring cellular morphology, behavior, and dynamic biological processes.

FRAP

Fluorescence recovery after photobleaching for investigating molecular mobility, diffusion, and fluorescence dynamics.

Three-Dimensional Imaging

Z-stack acquisition and optical sectioning for three-dimensional visualization of cells, tissues, and biological structures.

Live-Cell Imaging

Silicone Immersion for Live-Cell Imaging

Silicone immersion objective for high-resolution live-cell imaging.

High-resolution imaging of living samples

Silicone immersion objectives provide a refractive index closer to living biological samples, helping reduce spherical aberration during imaging at greater depths.

Silicone immersion objectives are well suited for live-cell and live-tissue imaging, particularly when extended observation and high-resolution imaging are required.

Fluorescence Dynamics

FRAP & Molecular Mobility

Measuring fluorescence recovery

Fluorescence recovery after photobleaching can be used to investigate the mobility and exchange of fluorescently labeled molecules within living cells.

FRAP experiments can provide information about molecular diffusion, mobile and immobile fractions, and the dynamics of fluorescently labeled structures.
