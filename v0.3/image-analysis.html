Beyond Acquisition

From Microscopy Images
to Biological Insight

Microscopy datasets often contain information that cannot be fully captured by visual inspection alone. Image analysis can transform complex images into measurable features, trajectories, structures, and quantitative datasets that help answer biological questions.

What We Analyze

Image Analysis Capabilities

Analysis workflows can be adapted to the imaging modality, biological structure, dataset size, and quantitative question.

01

Image Processing

Prepare microscopy datasets for visualization and downstream analysis.

  • Background and shading correction
  • Denoising and image enhancement
  • Deconvolution
  • Registration and stitching
  • Z-stack and multichannel processing
02

Segmentation

Identify and separate biological structures within microscopy images.

  • Cell and nuclear segmentation
  • Organelle segmentation
  • Object detection
  • Instance segmentation
  • AI-assisted segmentation
03

Tracking

Follow cells, organelles, particles, and other biological structures through time.

  • Cell migration
  • Cell division and lineage
  • Organelle movement
  • Particle tracking
  • Trajectory analysis
04

3D Analysis

Extract structural and spatial information from volumetric microscopy datasets.

  • 3D reconstruction
  • Surface rendering
  • Volume and surface measurements
  • Object morphology
  • Spatial relationships
05

Quantification

Convert image features into numerical measurements for statistical analysis.

  • Intensity measurements
  • Area and volume
  • Object counting
  • Morphological parameters
  • Colocalization analysis
06

AI-Assisted Analysis

Machine-learning approaches can assist with segmentation and analysis of complex microscopy datasets.

  • Cell and nucleus detection

  • Custom training workflows

Quantitative Microscopy

What Can Be Measured?

Depending on the biological question, microscopy images can be converted into a wide range of quantitative measurements.

Biological Feature Example Measurements
Cells Number, area, morphology, density
Nuclei Area, volume, intensity, shape
Fluorescence Mean intensity, integrated intensity, intensity distribution
Objects Number, size, shape, spatial distribution
Time-Lapse Speed, displacement, trajectory, duration, event frequency
Spatial Relationships Distance, overlap, colocalization
