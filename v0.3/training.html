Optimize Your Experiment

Good Imaging Starts Before Acquisition

Imaging parameters can strongly affect the quality and interpretation of biological data. Planning the acquisition strategy in advance can help avoid unnecessary photobleaching, phototoxicity, saturation, and poor signal-to-noise.

  • Define the biological question
  • Select an appropriate imaging approach
  • Optimize acquisition parameters
  • Consider photobleaching and phototoxicity
  • Plan image analysis and quantitative measurements

Advanced Imaging

Application-Specific Guidance

For more specialized experiments, consultation can focus on the imaging strategy required by the biological application.

Live-Cell Imaging

Time-lapse design, environmental control, imaging frequency, phototoxicity, and long-term image quality.

Quantitative Fluorescence

Acquisition considerations for fluorescence intensity measurements and quantitative comparisons.

FRAP

Experimental design and acquisition considerations for studying molecular mobility and fluorescence recovery.

FLIM

Guidance for fluorescence lifetime measurements and application-specific imaging strategies.

Multichannel Imaging

Channel selection, spectral separation, signal optimization, and minimizing cross-talk between fluorophores.

Image Analysis Planning

Consider how images will be quantified before acquisition so that imaging parameters support downstream analysis.
